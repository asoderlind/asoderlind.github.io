It's hard to stop when you start building on a homelab. There are always things to add. I've already seen the full pipeline from lurking on reddit. People who start by having a single little mini-pc on the desk, whirring away in the corner, to building a small-scale data center in the middle of their garage. Personally I'm not there yet, but we will see what the future holds.

My latest addition is a managed unifi switch: a [UbiQuiti USW-LITE-8-POE](https://amzn.to/4xnZNK2)

![Image of a UbiQuiti USW-LITE-8-POE]({{ '/assets/images/homelab/unifi-switch.png' | relative_url }})

It's nice to have a managed switch because it allows me to connect all the computers in the rack with a wired connection to the internet, for maximum speed, and it also allows for more sophisticated segregation and monitoring.

I also added a [GeeekPi 12-Port Patch Panel](https://amzn.to/4gmGT0k) since the switch faces forward but some of the ports on the computers, and the incoming ethernet cable are at the back.

![Image of a GeeekPi 12-Port Patch Panel]({{ '/assets/images/homelab/patch-panel.png' | relative_url }})

Finally I chose to order another 10-inch rack, a [DeskPi RackMate T1](https://amzn.to/3TCOEqn) this time, which I decided to stack on top of the old one, cobbled together for now but with proper screws on the way.

![Image of a 10 inch server rack with computers, cable and a network switch]({{ '/assets/images/homelab/deskpi-setup-v2.jpg' | relative_url }})

The main purpose the whole stack serves is still a personal Nextcloud instance, served through a private tunnel, but I have also started using [Kavita](https://github.com/Kareadita/Kavita) more and more for reading on my iPad. I still haven't gotten around to installing tailscale on my Kobo but that will be a future post. Right now I am reading "Laziness does not exist" by Devon Price, and I am enjoying it very much. I will also make a post with my thoughts on the book after I finish it.
